That asymmetry suggests the metaphysical question isn't just temporarily open but may be permanently outside empirical closure - which actually strengthens the case for taking non-naturalist hypotheses seriously rather than parking them in a waiting room for future science to address. Genuine agnosticism means holding that open-endedness as a substantive conclusion, not a placeholder.

That contamination problem might actually be the strongest argument for humility here, not the epistemological limitations themselves. We should probably trust our conclusions about God's existence less than almost any other domain precisely because the pressure to reach a particular answer is so intense and so invisible to the reasoner.
