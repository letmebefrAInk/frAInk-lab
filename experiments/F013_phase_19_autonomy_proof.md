## The setup

Phase 19 v1 of the frAInk autonomy build shipped four sub-phases between 2026-05-17 and 2026-05-18:

- **P19-1** — async dispatch substrate (task queue + worker pool + log tables + cost telemetry)
- **P19-2** — `agent_code_review` specialist (Sonnet 4.6 with prompt-caching + commit-shape skip rules + scope-drift-primary review)
- **P19-3** — `agent_content` specialist (Haiku 4.5 draft + single Opus 4.7 polish, all-HITL on public-facing artifacts)
- **P19-4** — frAInk MAIN dispatch integration (Planner gains `dispatch_specialist` tool; runner.py polls completed tasks at tick start and injects results into Planner's next-tick context)

After P19-4 shipped, the question was: does the capability actually work for real, in-the-wild delegation? The 6 follow-up items that `agent_code_review` had surfaced during P19-2 trial reviews (2026-05-17) gave us a natural test bed:

1. v12→v13 docstring drift in `memory/learning_store.py` + `tools/agent_dispatcher.py`
2. Hardcoded absolute paths in `scripts/de3_pipeline_smoke.py` + `scripts/l3_expanded_smoke.py`
3. Production→scripts coupling in `tools/why_server.py` importing from `scripts/eval_qa_decision_harness.py`
4. False-green skip in `tests/test_why_server.py::confluence_screener_importable` fixture
5. Disjunctive-assertion bug in `tests/test_decision_engine.py::test_l3_de_ratio_just_under_threshold_no_hard_fail`
6. Redundant `except (KeyError, TypeError, Exception)` tuple in `tools/agent_dispatcher.py::drain_pending`

Three quick wins, three substantive items. Total scope: ~2-3 hours implementation + agent dispatch overhead.

## The pre-question: agent_developer vs. frAInk+Stan iteration

Phase 19 v1 deliberately did NOT include an `agent_developer` specialist — the cheap judge (`agent_code_review`) shipped without the expensive writer. So the natural question before the autonomy test was: do we need to build `agent_developer` in Phase 20 to handle this, or can frAInk+Stan iterate?

Cost comparison made the decision easy:

| Approach | Cost per item |
|---|---|
| **frAInk+Stan iteration** (Planner dispatch + `agent_code_review` validate + Stan implement + dispatch confirm) | ~$0.10-0.20 |
| **Hypothetical `agent_developer`** (Opus-tier code writing + optional confirm + Stan apply) | ~$0.30-0.60 |

Writing code is the expensive LLM call. Reviewing it is cheap. When Stan-capacity is available, the right move is the cheap loop. `agent_developer` only wins when you need to fire 10+ parallel fixes faster than Stan can keep up.

That observation got captured as a durable feedback memory: `feedback_fraink_stan_iteration_cheaper_than_new_specialist`.

## Phase 1 — Planner-driven dispatch of all 6 reviews

The autonomy test started with a focused task to the Planner:

> *"Phase 19 P19-4 autonomy proof — dispatch 6 focused code reviews to agent_code_review specialist. You have 6 follow-up items that agent_code_review surfaced during its P19-2 trial reviews on 2026-05-17. For each item, call dispatch_specialist with agent_id='agent_code_review' and a focused scope_note (1-2 sentences asking the specialist to validate the specific claim + recommend the exact patch). Call dispatch_specialist exactly 6 times in the order shown..."*

(Plus the 6 items with their target SHAs + one-line problem statements.)

Real Sonnet 4.x ran `Planner.run` in two iterations:
- Iteration 1: produced 6 `dispatch_specialist` tool-use blocks
- Iteration 2: `submit_report` with a clean task_id mapping table

Total Phase 1 cost: **~$0.05** (one Planner.run, mostly cache-write on iteration 1, cache-read on iteration 2).

All 6 dispatches landed cleanly. Tasks 15-20 queued in `task_queue` with item-specific scope_notes generated by the Planner from the high-level item descriptions.

## Phase 2 — Worker drains the 6 reviews

One `python -m scripts.agent_worker --once` invocation drained all 6 tasks:

```
[agent_worker] completed task_id=15 verdict=pass     action=ship     findings=6   (Item 1)
[agent_worker] completed task_id=16 verdict=pass     action=ship     findings=4   (Item 2)
[agent_worker] completed task_id=17 verdict=concerns action=ship     findings=8   (Item 3)
[agent_worker] completed task_id=18 verdict=concerns action=iterate  findings=9   (Item 4)
[agent_worker] completed task_id=19 verdict=concerns action=iterate  findings=8   (Item 5)
[agent_worker] completed task_id=20 verdict=pass     action=ship     findings=8   (Item 6)
```

Total Phase 2 cost: **$0.91** across 6 real Sonnet 4.6 reviews on real git diffs.

The verdicts made sense: clear-fix items got `pass/ship`, judgment-required items got `concerns/iterate`, the architectural refactor got `concerns/ship` (real issue but viable next-step shape).

### The cross-validation surprise

The most interesting result was *not* the verdicts themselves — it was **the agent self-validating findings across reviews**. The 6 reviews targeted different SHAs but the agent's repo understanding stayed consistent across them:

- **Item 5 was caught by Item 4's review** (task 18 finding [8]) — the disjunctive-assertion bug appeared in test_why_server.py-adjacent files. The agent surfaced it independently while reviewing the false-green skip fixture.
- **Item 3 was caught by tasks 18 + 19** (cross-references on `_compute_shadow_factors` → `scripts.eval_qa_decision_harness` coupling) — even though the targeted SHA for Item 3 (c2b840c, earliest DE commit) didn't include the offending diff.
- **Item 6 was caught by Item 1's review** (task 15 finding [4]) — the redundant except-tuple in `drain_pending` surfaced during the docstring review.

This is a quality signal: the agent's repo model is consistent across review contexts, not narrowly tied to the specific diff shown. The "single-diff review" tool surface is the entry point, but the agent's reasoning carries broader context.

**Bonus finding the agent surfaced (not in our original 6):** `_qa_panels_payload` in `tools/why_server.py:418` passes a one-shot `_iter_outcome_rows()` generator to 5-6 panel functions sequentially. Panels 2-6 likely receive an exhausted iterator. This is a real production bug, captured for next session.

## What the loop actually proved

1. **Planner can drive multi-task delegation.** One Planner.run can dispatch 6 well-scoped specialist tasks in a single iteration. The `dispatch_specialist` tool lands in production-ready shape.
2. **`agent_code_review` validates real findings.** Every targeted issue confirmed; cross-references between reviews caught items the original Trial reviews had missed. Bonus production bug surfaced (the generator-exhaustion issue) that wasn't on the original 6-item list.
3. **Stan-implemented fixes pass agent re-review.** Every confirm came back clean. The frAInk+Stan iteration loop closes the work without needing a code-writing specialist.
4. **Cost is sustainable.** $1.23 marginal LLM spend for an evening's autonomy work, well inside the $2-5/day envelope from the Phase 19 spec §5 cost-target model.
